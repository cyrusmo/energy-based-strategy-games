\begin{abstract}
We present an early research system for separating high-level strategy generation from low-level action execution in adversarial multi-agent environments.
The system combines an energy-based model over latent strategy embeddings, approximate sampled-response evaluation, a strategy-conditioned policy, and lightweight update hooks for policy learning, contrastive energy learning, and one-step world-model fitting.
The current contribution is a complete methods scaffold on a redesigned gridworld: continuous energy-based strategy generation, exact finite-horizon Nash/best-response evaluation, and conference baselines (PPO, diversity-only, PSRO-lite).
Gate 1 is passed (non-degenerate strategic trade-offs). Gate 2 currently fails, so we report the system as methodological and diagnostic rather than as a positive transfer result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Multi-agent reinforcement learning systems often entangle strategic intent and low-level control in a single policy.
This makes it difficult to inspect whether failure comes from poor strategy generation, weak opponent evaluation, brittle execution, or unstable learning updates.
We study a decomposed alternative: generate candidate latent strategies, evaluate them against sampled opponent responses, execute the selected strategy with a conditioned policy, and update the components from rollout data.

The guiding research question is whether high-level strategies can be generated, evaluated, selected, and executed as a distinct layer above low-level policies.
This paper frames the contribution as an inspectable methods scaffold rather than a performance benchmark.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Hierarchical and option-based RL.}
The options framework~\cite{sutton1999options} introduces temporal abstraction through macro-actions, but options are typically hand-designed or discovered without adversarial evaluation.
Skill-discovery methods such as DIAYN~\cite{eysencbach2018diayn} and variational intrinsic control~\cite{gregor2016vic} learn diverse behaviors via mutual-information objectives, yet they do not score skills against adversaries or compute game-theoretic quantities.
Universal value functions~\cite{schaul2015uvfa} condition policies on goals; our embeddings encode \emph{how} to behave rather than \emph{what} to achieve, and the evaluator explicitly models adversarial responses.

\paragraph{Population-based methods.}
Policy-Space Response Oracles (PSRO)~\cite{lanctot2017psro} maintain a discrete population and compute a meta-Nash equilibrium, providing rigorous game-theoretic grounding at the cost of a growing policy set.
League training in AlphaStar~\cite{vinyals2019alphastar} and Nash averaging~\cite{balduzzi2019openspiel} similarly evaluate agents against a constructed population.
DeepNash~\cite{perolat2022deepnash} and $\alpha$-Rank~\cite{omidshafiei2019alpharank} scale equilibrium computation, but still operate over discrete policy sets.
Our generator can be viewed as a continuous proposal distribution that supplies candidates to a PSRO-like evaluator.

\paragraph{Regret minimization.}
Counterfactual regret minimization~\cite{zinkevich2007cfr} and the Hart--Mas-Colell procedure~\cite{hart2000ce} converge to Nash or correlated equilibria by iteratively reducing regret.
We treat the energy model as a continuous relaxation: high-exploitability strategies receive high energy and therefore vanishing probability mass under $p_\psi$.

\paragraph{Latent-conditioned policies and implicit models.}
Goal-conditioned RL and implicit behavioral cloning~\cite{florence2022ibc} show that energy-like models can represent multi-modal action distributions.
We lift that idea from actions to strategy embeddings, so sampling explores behavioral modes rather than one-step actions.

\paragraph{Energy-based models and world models.}
Energy-based generative models~\cite{du2019implicit,grathwohl2020jem} capture multi-modal densities without a normalized decoder, and have been used for model-based planning~\cite{du2019ebmplanning}.
Dreamer~\cite{hafner2020dreamer} and MuZero~\cite{schrittwieser2020muzero} learn predictive models for planning but do not compute opponent best responses.
Our world model is an efficiency component for imagined rollouts; reported exploitability uses exact dynamic programming on the finite gridworld.
PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} is the monolithic policy-gradient baseline.
Recent open-ended search systems~\cite{novikov2025alphaevolve} motivate treating strategy invention as a generative problem rather than a single-policy optimization.


\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
Consider a two-player finite-horizon zero-sum game with state space $\mathcal{S}$, action spaces $\mathcal{A}_1,\mathcal{A}_2$, and attacker reward $R$.
A strategy $\sigma\in\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ parameterizes a family of policies $\pi_\theta(a\mid s,\sigma)$.
The attacker value is $V(\sigma^{(1)},\sigma^{(2)})=\mathbb{E}[\sum_t \gamma^t R_t]$.
Exact exploitability of $\sigma$ is $V_{\mathrm{Nash}}-V(\sigma,\mathrm{BR}(\sigma))$, computed by backward induction on Gridworld v2.
The sampled response gap is a cheap training diagnostic and is never reported as exploitability.

\subsection{Generate, Evaluate, Execute, Update}

At each iteration the system executes the following loop.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Generate}: sample latent strategy embeddings from an energy model using Langevin dynamics, with at most one named heuristic as an optional anchor.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate}: score candidates with $G(\sigma)=\alpha V(\sigma,\mathrm{BR}(\sigma))+(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}[V]$ against scripted defenders; exact BR is available for reported numbers.
  \item \textbf{Execute}: map $\sigma$ through a parametric controller or a strategy-conditioned policy.
  \item \textbf{Update}: store rollout summaries in a strategy buffer and apply policy, contrastive-divergence EBM, and one-step world-model updates.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Energy-Based Strategy Generator}

Let $z\in\mathbb{R}^d$ denote a latent strategy embedding and $E_\psi(z)$ a scalar energy.
Candidates are sampled by unadjusted Langevin dynamics
\[
z_{t+1}=z_t-\frac{\eta}{2}\nabla_z E_\psi(z_t)+\epsilon_t,\qquad \epsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\eta I).
\]
Contrastive divergence uses high-scoring buffer strategies as positives and Langevin samples as negatives.
The first six coordinates of $z$ are interpretable controller weights (directness, evasiveness, aggression, patience, feint, commit time), so arbitrary latents are executable without snapping to a 5-class lookup.

\subsection{Exact and sampled evaluation}

Training-time selection may use sampled opponents.
Reported exploitability uses dynamic programming: a minimizing defender against a fixed attacker policy, and a per-state $5\times 5$ Nash LP for the game value.
The world model is an efficiency component and is not the source of reported best responses.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Primary Environment}

The paper testbed is Gridworld v2: a $7\times 7$ attacker--defender game with a split wall, two scoring goals, and named layouts in which \texttt{evasive}, \texttt{feint}, and \texttt{patient} are each uniquely best.
v1 (open $10\times 10$, one goal) is retained only to reproduce the August 2026 diagnostic snapshot.
Exact Nash values are tractable because the joint state $(p_{\mathrm{att}},p_{\mathrm{def}},t)$ has on the order of $10^4$--$10^5$ elements.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare the strategy loop against PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo}, a diversity-only Gaussian sampler without adversarial scoring, restricted PSRO~\cite{lanctot2017psro} over the named strategy set, and the direct-goal heuristic.
PPO is evaluated on the same held-out layouts and against exact best-response defenders.

\subsection{Ablations}

The paper-facing ablation suite covers EBM versus Gaussian sampling, buffer positives versus random positives, robustness-aware versus average-value selection, candidate count, sampled opponent count, and world-model fitting on or off.

\section{Limitations}

Exact exploitability is available on Gridworld v2 but expensive if a mixed Nash LP is solved at every joint state; reported training loops therefore select with sampled $G$ and compute exact numbers on demand.
The learned world model is not yet the best-response engine.
Multi-evader pursuit, $n$-player CCE, and a proof of the energy-mass-on-equilibria conjecture are left to future work.
