# Research Snapshot: Energy-Guided Weight-Space Repair

Date: 2026-11-20

Status: pilot. Tables are generated by `examples/run_energy_repair.py`
(schema `energy_repair/v1`, seeds 0-19) into `outputs/public/energy_repair/`.

## Question

Can an energy model guide a lasting edit to a policy's weights: high energy
marking what needs repair, and low-energy regions marking what must not move?

## Result

Energy-guided edits recovered all of the return lost to a planted fault
(98-102% across four fault types) while 2.9-4.5% of held-out positions on the
other layouts lost more than 0.10 return at the worst point of the edit path.
Selecting edits for repair alone gave the same recovery with 14-18% peak harm.

The comparison that should not be over-read is the position-blind control. It
did much worse here (83-88% recovery, 16-17.5% harm), but part of that is how
this protocol flags positions (see caveats).

## Setup

- **Positions.** All 9,480 non-terminal (attacker cell, defender cell) starts
  on the five Gridworld v2 layouts.
- **Returns.** Exact. Each of the five named attacker strategies is rolled out
  to termination from each position against each of the five scripted
  defenders, and the return is the mean over defenders.
- **Selector.** A 3,413-weight MLP mapping position features plus a layout
  one-hot to a mix over strategies. A position's return is the mix-weighted
  strategy return.
- **Energy.** `EnergyMLP` over (position, strategy embedding), trained to rank
  strategies by return. The energy of a decision is its expected excess over
  the lowest-energy strategy at that position.
- **Split, per seed.** 70% of positions fit the selector and the energy, 10%
  are the evidence the search sees, 20% are held out for every number below.
- **Faults.** Fine-tune all weights so the selector overuses one strategy on
  one layout (layout = seed mod 5). `blanket` forces it at every position;
  `selective` only where that strategy loses more than 0.10 return.
  `aggressive` is almost never uniquely right; `feint` is uniquely right at
  roughly 15% of positions. Both were chosen from layout statistics before any
  repair was run.
- **Search.** 40 steps; each step edits 32 weight coordinates and picks a step
  size from a fixed grid. The quarter of evidence positions with the highest
  decision energy is flagged for repair.
- **Rotation.** A random orthogonal change of weight coordinates. The policy
  is identical, but no coordinate belongs to a single layout any more.

| Method | Guidance signal | Protects unflagged positions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `energy_guided` | learned energy | yes |
| `repair_only` | learned energy | no |
| `position_blind` | learned energy averaged over positions (one score per strategy) | yes |
| `oracle_regret` | true regret | yes |

Mean return on the other layouts rose under `energy_guided` (+0.012 to +0.023)
and fell under `position_blind` (-0.041 to -0.049). `repair_only` ranged from
-0.025 to +0.005.

Rotation left recovery intact and raised `energy_guided` peak harm by a factor
of 1.3 to 2.4. It lowered `repair_only` harm, so the gap between the two
narrowed from roughly 4-6x to under 2x.

## Caveats

1. **The energy model is a better policy than the selector.** Acting on the
   lowest-energy strategy scores 0.700 on held-out positions (seeds 0-4); the
   clean selector scores 0.663 and the per-position best is 0.735. The energy
   was trained on exact returns for 70% of positions, including the faulted
   layout. The repair is therefore distillation from an intact teacher into a
   damaged student through sparse edits. Recovery above 100% is the same fact.
2. **Harm is not purely collateral.** Flagging is global: the top quarter of
   all evidence positions, while the fault touches at most a fifth. The search
   deliberately edits other layouts where the clean selector was already
   wrong. Harm counts the positions that got worse; the net change there was
   positive.
3. **The position-blind control is handicapped by the same quota.** With one
   score per strategy it must flag a quarter of positions by behaviour alone,
   which pushes every layout toward the on-average-best strategy. Its harm is
   flat across coordinates and fault types, consistent with the quota rather
   than the fault driving it. A blind control restricted to the faulted layout
   was not run.
4. **`oracle_regret` is not scale-matched.** True regret is about ten times
   smaller than learned energy, and the quadratic preservation penalty is not
   rescaled, so this arm protects unflagged positions less. It is not an upper
   bound.
5. **Dev seeds 100-103 shaped the protocol.** Two changes came from them: the
   energy of a decision became excess over the per-position minimum, and the
   preservation penalty became quadratic. Seeds 0-19 were run once afterwards.
6. **Scope.** One-shot strategy selection against scripted defenders on a 7x7
   grid, not exact exploitability. The selector does not generalise well: the
   clean selector is materially suboptimal at roughly a fifth of held-out
   positions before any fault.

## Next

- Energy trained without labels from the faulted layout.
- Scale-matched oracle.
- Faults planted in a selector whose weights were never trained with the
  layout one-hot, so no layout-specific coordinate exists even before rotation.
